## Meeting Notes

### This Week in HLSL

There were 35 PRs merged in HLSL-related areas this week.

* The HLSL Frontend saw some groupshared related fixes, a bug fix related to
  boolean comparisons, and further work on semantics.
* Further work on semantics went on in the DirectX backend, as well as
  improvements to GEP handling in DXILResourceAccess.
* The SPIR-V backend had various improvements to debug info, a fix for type
  reconstruction of aggregates, and tightening of the SPIR-V extensions used
  for Vulkan.
* In the offload suite, some tests were moved to HLSL 2021 for portability,
  more semantic support was added, and various XFAILs were updated.

21 new PRs were opened, and there are 51 ongoing PRs.

For a full list of merged PRs, see the appendix.

### Planning Update from NVIDIA

Diego gave a brief overview of NVIDIA's interests in HLSL in clang. They're
interested in clang as a replacement for DXC for graphics in Vulkan, and hope
to get a few sample apps working by end of year so that they can share a
preview of clang-dxc further with partners. A lot of this is related to
improving debug info, as clang is already better at targeting SPIR-V's
NonSemantic DebugInfo than dxc since it doesn't need to go through spirv-opt.

We discussed coordination across organizations with respect to issues being
worked on and agreed that issue labels (HLSL, SPIR-V, etc) and assigning issues
that are being worked on are sufficient communication here.

Damyan asked about the apps being targeted for NVIDIA's milestone to see if it
would be interesting to look at them from a DirectX perspective as well, and
Diego pointed at https://github.com/google/bigwheels - specifically the
`fishtornado` and `fluid_simulation` samples.

### Expectations Around Post-Merge Build Failures

Dan Brown asked about expectations around build failures when merging PRs. We
follow the general LLVM policy here, where it's the committer's responsibility
to exercise due diligence on bot failures, and to follow a revert-to-green
policy. When putting up a new PR that includes a fix for the issue, it's best
to refer to the previous PR, point out what's different in the new change, and
include the original reviewers of the change so that the new review will be
expedient.
